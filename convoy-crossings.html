The twelve great convoys — crossings through the year

Every meeting each great convoy holds, laid on the calendar, so the spacing can be read directly. A dot is a crossing; its colour is where the meeting was held and its size is how many hulls were there — or, if you switch the encoding, what it cost. The line between two dots is the wait, and each convoy’s longest wait is drawn heavy and labelled in days. The meter on the right is the part that bites: days of layover spent on meetings, out of the layover the circuit leaves. The darker band at the end of each meter is the reserve — ten days the Office will not book away, so no crew's whole year goes on meetings. No convoy holds two meetings inside a fortnight, so every one of them has time to be got ready for and time for something to happen after.

Read off inputs-v4.json by the same model that draws the chart of the Settled Sphere. No convoy holds two meetings inside a fortnight, and it may not meet the same convoy twice inside three weeks. Twelve is the ceiling, but what actually decides how many a convoy holds is the money: a crossing costs it four days when the venue is on its lane and up to nine when it is not, and the Office will not book it past the reserve. So the roads with the longest circuits hold the fewest meetings, which is geography rather than policy.
